How to plan a house move with ChatGPT: a moving checklist that fits your date

Plan a calmer move with AI: a week-by-week moving checklist built around your date, a room-by-room packing plan, address changes and a first-night box.

Moving house is one of those jobs that looks manageable from a distance and then arrives all at once. There's the packing, the van or the removal firm, the meter readings, the address changes, the keys, and somewhere in the middle of it all your normal life still needs running. Generic moving checklists help a little, but they rarely match your date, your home or the people you're moving with.

An AI assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini can turn the generic list into your list. It doesn't know your tenancy terms, your mortgage timeline or the rules where you live, and it can't book anything for you. What it does well is the planning: working backwards from a date, breaking big jobs into small ones and keeping track of what's left. Below are five prompts that take you from "we're moving" to a plan you can actually follow. Replace everything in [brackets] with your own details.

Before you start: write down what you know

The more real detail you give the assistant, the more useful the plan. Take five minutes to note your moving date (or the earliest and latest it could be), the size of your current home, who's moving with you, including children and pets, whether you're renting or buying at either end, and how you plan to move: a removal firm, a hired van with friends, or a mix. Note anything awkward too, such as stairs with no lift, a narrow street or a piano.

You don't need to share your full address, contract details or account numbers. "A two-bedroom flat on the third floor, no lift" is plenty for a good plan.

1. Build a week-by-week moving timeline

The single most useful thing you can do is work backwards from moving day. Most of the stress in a move comes from jobs that needed starting three weeks earlier, like booking a van or giving notice. A timeline puts those early jobs where they belong.

I'm moving from [current home, e.g. a two-bedroom flat, third floor, no lift] to [new home] on [date], which is [number] weeks from today. I'm moving with [who, including children and pets] and plan to use [removal firm / hired van and friends]. Build a week-by-week moving checklist working backwards from that date. Put jobs that need booking or notice at the top of the early weeks, keep each week to a realistic number of tasks for someone who also works [full-time / part-time], and finish with a short list for moving day itself.

Read the timeline and adjust anything that doesn't fit your life. If a week looks crowded, ask the assistant to spread the load. Then copy the result into whatever you already use, a notes app, a shared document or a paper list on the fridge, so everyone in the household can see it.

2. Make a room-by-room packing plan

Packing goes faster when you start with the rooms you use least and leave the kitchen and bathroom until the end. It also goes faster when there's less to pack. This prompt gives you an order to work in, a rough box count and a gentle push to let go of things first.

Here are the rooms and storage spaces in my current home: [list them, e.g. bedroom, children's room, living room, kitchen, bathroom, hallway cupboard, loft]. My moving date is [date]. Suggest the order to pack them in, starting with what I use least, and give a rough estimate of how many boxes each might need. For each room, list three things I should decide whether to keep, sell, donate or throw away before I pack, and tell me which everyday items to leave out until the last two days.

Treat the box estimates as a starting point, not a shopping list. It's easier to buy a few more boxes later than to store a pile you didn't need. If decluttering feels like the bigger job, our Tidy Home Planner has prompts for working through a home one small area at a time.

3. Set up a labelling system you'll thank yourself for

On the day, nobody remembers what's in box 23. A simple labelling system means the right boxes land in the right rooms and you can find the kettle, the chargers and the children's favourite toys without opening everything.

Design a simple labelling system for my move. I'm moving into [new home, e.g. a three-bedroom house with two floors]. Give each room in the new home a short name and a colour or number, and suggest what to write on every box (room, a few words on the contents, and whether it should be opened first). Then make a short inventory template I can fill in as I pack, with columns for box number, room, contents and priority.

Stick a matching label or sign on each door of the new home before the boxes arrive. It takes ten minutes and saves helpers from asking "where does this go?" forty times.

4. List every address change and account to update

Updating your address is the job that keeps popping up for months after a move. A quiet afternoon with a list catches most of it in one go. The assistant can help you think of the places you'd otherwise forget.

I'm moving on [date] and live in [country]. Help me build a checklist of everyone who may need my new address or a final meter reading, grouped into: home services (energy, water, internet, insurance), money (bank, cards, pensions, tax), health (doctor, dentist, pharmacy), work and education, subscriptions and online shops, and people (family, friends, clubs). For each group, add a column for "when to tell them" (before, on, or after moving day). Mark anything that usually needs notice in advance, and remind me to check the official rules and deadlines where I live, such as registering a change of address.

Rules about registering an address, transferring utilities and giving notice differ between countries and contracts, and the assistant may be out of date. Use its list to make sure nothing is forgotten, then check the details with the official service or the company itself.

5. Pack a first-night box and plan the first week

The first evening in a new home is easier when you're not hunting for a towel, a light bulb or the cat food. One clearly marked box, plus a short plan for the first few days, makes the arrival feel much calmer.

Make a first-night box list for my move. We are [who's moving, including ages of children and any pets], and we arrive at the new home around [time]. Include essentials for the evening, the night and the next morning, and keep it to what fits in one or two boxes I'll carry myself rather than put in the van. Then suggest a gentle plan for the first week in the new home: which rooms to unpack first, a few small jobs to do each day, and one or two ways to start feeling settled in the neighbourhood.

Put the first-night box in your own car or keep it with you, and tell everyone helping that it isn't to be stacked with the others. Many people also find it helps to make up the beds before they unpack anything else.

Small habits that keep a move calm

  • Keep one conversation for the move. Paste in updates as things change, such as a new date or a confirmed van, and ask the assistant to revise the plan, so it always has the full picture.
  • Do a little every day. Two boxes an evening for three weeks is far easier than a whole weekend of packing.
  • Photograph before you unplug. A quick photo of the cables behind the TV or router makes setting them up again much quicker.
  • Write down the readings. Take dated photos of the meters at both homes on moving day.

Where to go from here
