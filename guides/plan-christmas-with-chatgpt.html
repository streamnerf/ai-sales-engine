How to plan Christmas with ChatGPT: 5 calm prompts

Plan the holidays early with AI: a week-by-week calendar, a realistic budget, a gift list, a make-ahead menu and a cooking timeline. Five prompts to copy.

December has a way of arriving all at once. One week it's autumn, the next you're buying wrapping paper at nine in the evening, trying to remember whether you promised to bring dessert or host the whole thing. Most of the stress isn't the celebrating itself. It's the pile of small decisions that nobody made early: who's getting what, what it will all cost, who cooks, and when anyone is supposed to rest.

An AI assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini is surprisingly good at exactly this kind of planning. It won't know your family, but it's patient, organised and happy to turn a messy list into a calendar, a budget or a shopping list. Do this in October or November and the season gets noticeably calmer. Below are five prompts you can copy, fill in and adjust. Replace everything in [brackets] with your own details.

Before you start: decide what you actually want from the season

A plan is only as good as the goal behind it. Before you open a chat, take five minutes with a notebook, or ask the people you live with, and answer three questions:

  • What made last year good? A particular meal, a walk, an evening with nothing planned. These are the things to protect.
  • What made it hard? Too many visits in one weekend, overspending, one person doing all the cooking. These are the things to change.
  • What are the fixed points? Travel dates, school events, work days, the dinner that always happens at a relative's house.

Paste your answers into the first prompt. You don't need to share names or personal details; "my sister's family of four" works just as well as their actual names.

1. Build a week-by-week holiday calendar

The single most useful thing you can do is see the whole season on one page. When everything lives in your head, every task feels urgent at once. When it's spread across weeks, with deadlines and gaps, most of it turns out to be perfectly manageable.

Help me plan the holiday season from [start date] to [end date] for a household of [who lives with you]. Fixed events: [list dates and events]. Last year the hard parts were [list], and the parts we want to protect are [list]. Build a week-by-week calendar that spreads out gift shopping, card writing, food prep, cleaning and travel, with a clear deadline for each. Leave at least one quiet evening each week and a buffer day before every big event. Flag any week that looks overloaded.

Read the result critically. If the assistant has put three tasks on the evening you always work late, tell it: "Thursdays are impossible, move those." Two or three rounds of this and you'll have a plan that fits your real life rather than an ideal one. Copy the final version into your calendar or a shared note so everyone at home can see it.

2. Set a realistic holiday budget

Holiday spending tends to creep up through lots of small purchases rather than one big one: an extra gift here, a second food shop there, decorations you didn't plan to buy. Deciding on a total first, then splitting it, makes each of those small choices easier.

My total budget for the holidays is [amount and currency]. Split it into gifts, food and drink, travel, decorations, social events and a buffer of about 10 percent. Here's what I usually buy: [rough list]. Point out the categories where people commonly overspend without noticing, and suggest two or three ways to stay within each one that don't feel stingy. Then give me a simple table I can use to track what I actually spend each week.

The assistant can't see your bank account, so its suggestions are only as accurate as the numbers you give it. Use last year's statements as a rough guide if you have them. If money is tight this year, say so plainly in the prompt; it will suggest gentler options such as a price cap, homemade gifts or a shared meal instead of hosting. Keep account numbers and other sensitive financial details out of the chat. If your wider monthly budget needs some attention too, our guide to making a simple monthly budget with ChatGPT is a good next step.

3. Make a thoughtful gift list (and simplify the gift-giving)

Gift lists are where AI saves the most time, because most of the work is brainstorming. Give it one honest line about each person, a budget, and it will suggest ideas you probably wouldn't have thought of. The trick is to describe people by what they enjoy and need, not by age and gender.

I need gifts for these people: [for each person, one line on what they enjoy, what they already have plenty of, and roughly what I'd like to spend]. Suggest three ideas per person: one physical gift, one experience, and one homemade or low-cost option. Avoid generic gift-set ideas. Then write a short, friendly message I could send to [family or friend group] proposing a simpler plan this year, such as [secret Santa / gifts for the kids only / a price cap of amount], without it sounding like I'm cutting back on them.

Treat the ideas as a starting point and check prices and availability yourself before buying, since the assistant's product knowledge can be out of date. The message at the end is often the most valuable part. Plenty of families would welcome a simpler arrangement; someone just has to suggest it first, kindly.

4. Plan a make-ahead holiday menu and shopping list

If you're hosting, the menu decides how your day feels. A menu where almost everything is cooked on the day means you spend the celebration in the kitchen. A menu built around dishes that can be prepared a day or two ahead means you get to sit down with everyone else.

I'm hosting [number] people for [meal] on [date]. Dietary needs: [list, e.g. one vegetarian, one gluten-free, a toddler]. My kitchen has [oven, hob and fridge details]. Plan a menu where most dishes can be made one or two days ahead, with a vegetarian main that doesn't feel like an afterthought. Then write a shopping list grouped by store section, marking which items can be bought a week early and which need to be fresh.

If a guest offers to bring something, add it to the prompt and ask the assistant to rebalance the menu around it. Always double-check allergy information on actual product labels; an AI-generated list is a helpful draft, not a guarantee that something is safe for a particular guest.

5. Work backwards with a cooking timeline

The question that causes the most panic on the day is "what should I be doing right now?" A timeline that works backwards from serving time answers it before you start.

Create a cooking timeline for this menu: [list dishes]. We want to eat at [time]. I have [one oven / two ovens], [number] hob rings and [number] people who can help. Work backwards from serving time, include what to do the day before, and show oven temperatures and when each dish goes in and comes out. Add a short list of jobs I can hand to helpers, and build in 20 minutes of slack before serving.

Print it, or put it on a tablet propped up somewhere you can see it. Cooking times and temperatures vary by recipe and oven, so check them against the recipes you're actually using. The value of the timeline is the order and the overlaps, not precision to the minute.

A few habits that keep the season calm

  • Start earlier than feels necessary. Planning in October or early November gives you time to change your mind, compare prices and spread costs out.
  • Share the load in writing. Ask the assistant to split your task list fairly between the adults at home, then put it somewhere everyone can see it. A written plan is much easier to discuss than a vague sense of who usually does what.
  • Keep one running chat or note. Store your calendar, budget and gift list in one place so you can come back and adjust instead of starting over.
  • Protect the quiet evenings. When the plan gets busy, those are the first thing people give up. They're also what most people remember fondly afterwards.
  • Review in January. Before you pack the decorations away, jot down what worked, what you spent and what to change. Next year's planning will take half the time.

Where to go from here

Try the calendar prompt this week, even if the holidays still feel far away. Once you can see the season laid out week by week, the budget and gift list tend to follow naturally, and the menu and timeline can wait until a couple of weeks before you host.
