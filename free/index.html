Free prompts

A few prompts to start with.

Short, working prompts you can copy and use today. Replace the [brackets] with your own details.

Plan a calmer week

Turn a messy to-do list into a realistic week with room to breathe.

Here is everything on my plate this week: [paste list]. I have about [hours] focused hours available. Sort the tasks into must do, should do and can wait. Then place the must-dos into a Monday to Friday plan with no more than three focused blocks a day, and leave one afternoon free. Tell me plainly what I should drop or postpone.

Write a hook for a short video

Get ten honest opening lines for a Reel or TikTok in a minute.

I'm making a 20-second video about [topic] for [audience]. Write 10 opening lines under 10 words each. Mix styles: a question, a surprising fact, a mistake people make, a quick before and after. No clickbait and no promises I can't keep. Mark the two you think are strongest and say why.
More like this: The Short Video Studio →

Tighten a product description

Make a vague product description clear and specific without hype.

Here is my product description: [paste]. Rewrite it in under 120 words for [type of buyer]. Lead with the problem it solves, then three concrete details (size, material, what's included, how it's used). Cut adjectives that don't add information. Keep a warm, plain tone and don't invent features.
More like this: The Product Page Workshop →

Match your CV to a job ad

See what a recruiter will look for and where your CV falls short.

Job ad: [paste]. My CV: [paste]. List the five things this employer cares about most, in order. For each, quote the line in my CV that best shows it, or say 'missing'. Then suggest one honest rewrite for each weak or missing point, using only facts already in my CV.
More like this: The Job Search Companion →

Reply to an unhappy customer

Draft a calm, human reply to a complaint that actually helps.

A customer wrote this: [paste message]. Our policy is: [refund or exchange policy]. Draft a reply under 120 words that thanks them, names the specific problem, says clearly what we will do and by when, and ends with one simple next step for them. Warm and direct, no corporate phrases, no over-apologising.
More like this: The Customer Support Desk →

Explain a spreadsheet formula

Understand a formula someone else wrote, or get one built for you.

I use [Excel / Google Sheets]. My data: column A is [description], column B is [description], starting on row 2. I want to [what you want to calculate]. Give me the formula, explain each part in one plain sentence, and point out one way it could break (blank cells, text instead of numbers) and how to guard against it.
